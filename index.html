Research Problem: Can LLMs Actually Reason?

Many LLMs are strong, yet standard single-pass answers can still fail on multi-step problems

  • Arithmetic or algebra slips in long chains
  • Logical inconsistencies between steps
  • Hallucinated or unreliable answers
  • Weak performance on complex multi-step problems
Illustrative example

“A shop sells pens at 12 for 90 tk. What do 18 pens cost?”

Flawed: 90 ÷ 12 = 7 → 7 × 18 = 126 ✗
Correct: 90 ÷ 12 = 7.5 → 7.5 × 18 = 135 ✓

One wrong intermediate step ruins the answer. (Constructed example, not a measured result.)

Research Motivation

Why does better reasoning matter?

  • Mathematics: multi-step problem solving
  • Programming: correct code generation
  • Science: structured, checkable analysis
  • Reliability: trustworthy AI applications
Central research question

How can we improve the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models through Reinforcement Learning and Test-Time Scaling?

Approach 1: Reinforcement Learning (RL)

Learn from feedback during training

  • Agent: the LLM · Environment: the task
  • Action: generating a response (tokens)
  • Reward: a score, e.g. 1 if the final answer is correct
  • Policy πθ: the model’s distribution over outputs
Analogy: a student practises maths problems, checks answers, and adjusts study habits.
J(θ) = Ey∼πθ(·|x)[ R(x, y) ]

Illustrative objective. θ: model parameters; x: question; y: response; R: reward. Training raises the expected reward.

PPO and GRPO are algorithms for this update. GRPO scores a group of sampled answers per question relative to each other, and features in recent reasoning-LLM work (e.g. DeepSeek-R1). RL is broader than RLHF: rewards can be rule-based (answer checkers) or learned.

Approach 2: Test-Time Scaling (TTS)

“Allocating additional computational resources during inference so that an LLM can spend more effort solving a problem.”

Question
→
N candidate solutionslonger reasoning / sampling
→
Verifier / votebest-of-N, search
→
Final answer
  • More reasoning tokens, multiple attempts, best-of-N
  • Search and verification over solution paths
  • Cost: more tokens, more latency
  • Parameters are not necessarily updated.
  • More compute ≠ guaranteed correctness
Ctotal = Cmodel + Creasoning + Csearch/verify

Conceptual, not universal. Cmodel: base forward cost; Creasoning: extra generated tokens; Csearch/verify: sampling and checking overhead.

Example: sample 5 solutions to the pen problem → 135, 135, 126, 135, 140. A vote or checker picks 135. Reasoning traces are not proof of the model’s internal reasoning.

Reinforcement Learning vs. Test-Time Scaling

Reinforcement LearningTest-Time Scaling
Main objectiveLearn better reasoning behaviourSpend more effort per problem
WhenTraining phaseInference phase
Parameters change?YesNot necessarily
ComputeHeavy upfront training costExtra cost on every query
How it may helpRewards reinforce correct reasoning patternsExploring and checking more candidates
LimitationsReward design, reward hackingLatency, cost, unreliable self-verification
RL = learning and practising before an examination.
TTS = spending more time and effort on a question during the examination.

Research Gap and Open Challenges

Potential gaps requiring further investigation, not claims of “unexplored” territory

  • High computational cost of test-time reasoning
  • Reward design and reward hacking in RL
  • Reliability of self-verification
  • Accuracy vs. inference-cost trade-offs
  • Are RL and TTS gains complementary?
  • Fair evaluation across models and benchmarks
Surveys (e.g. Pan et al.) already discuss several of these; this proposal focuses on a structured, cost-aware comparison at undergraduate scale.

Conclusion

Reinforcement Learning helps LLMs learn better reasoning behaviours, while Test-Time Scaling gives them additional computational effort to solve difficult problems. Studying both approaches provides an important direction for improving reasoning in modern deep learning systems.
